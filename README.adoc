= KaSH
:toc: macro
:toclevels: 2
:idprefix:
:idseparator: -
:icons: font
:source-highlighter: highlight.js

*Your messaging services in one window, each in its own isolated browser.*

KaSH shows the web versions of messaging services in tabs, in the manner of Ferdium.
Each service runs in a Chromium profile of its own: two services share nothing, not a cookie, not a cache entry, not an IndexedDB database, and each one keeps its data on disk, so it is still logged in at the next launch.
An unread badge on each tab, and the *Unread* tab, show what waits in every service: the *Unread* tab lists the conversations with unread messages, then the topics, the channels with new messages that their service does not count (in bold, without a mention).

== Services

WhatsApp, Google Chat, Slack, Google Messages, Telegram, Discord, Instagram, Messenger, LinkedIn and Microsoft Teams are in the catalog, as plugins: each one is a manifest, which describes the service and its options, and a script, which reads its unread conversations (see link:PLUGINS.adoc[the plugins' guide]).
What KaSH does for each of them, and for those to come, is in link:SERVICES.adoc[the services' table].
Other plugins are installed in Settings › Plugins, from a zip, a folder or an address, which KaSH can watch for a new version.
Any web application can also be added as a custom service, by its address.
A Slack service is one workspace, given by its name (`my-team` for `my-team.slack.com`).
A custom service shows its unread count when its page puts it at the start of its title, as `(3) My application` does.

KaSH reads WhatsApp's unread conversations: each one is a row of the *Unread* tab, which opens it in WhatsApp, and they make the badge of its tab.
As in WhatsApp, the muted and the archived conversations are not counted.

KaSH reads Google Chat's unread conversations the same way, as Google Chat counts them: the direct messages and the message requests, and the spaces where the user is mentioned.
And Slack's: the direct messages and the channels where the user is mentioned, without the muted ones; the other channels with new messages are topics.
Google Chat's spaces with new messages and no mention are topics too.
And Google Messages' (the SMS and RCS of an Android phone): its unread conversations, without the muted ones, each counted as one message, since Google Messages shows no count.
And Telegram's (Telegram Web K): its unread conversations, with their counts, without the muted ones and the archive.
And Discord's: its unread direct messages, with their counts; its servers with new messages are topics.
And Instagram's and Messenger's (Facebook's messages): their unread conversations, each counted as one message, since they show no count.
And LinkedIn's: its unread conversations, with their counts.
And Microsoft Teams' (teams.cloud.microsoft): its unread conversations, each counted as one message, since Teams shows no count; its Viva Engage communities with new posts are topics.

The search, the field in the middle of the title bar or ⌘⇧K, finds any contact or topic of every service by its name (its letters in order, whatever their accents), read or unread, and opens it in its service.
A service's page often shows only part of them (the rows of a list that fit, those it loaded): KaSH keeps those it saw, from a launch to the next.

The same service can be added several times, for several accounts: a label under its icon tells them apart.

Each service's "…" menu opens Chrome's DevTools on it, resets its cache, erases all its data (it is then logged out), or deletes it.
The tiles of the rail are dragged to change the services' order.
A right click on a service's tile in the rail opens a menu with all of it: back, forward, reload, mute its notifications, the "…" menu's actions, disable it and delete it.

A link to another site opens in the system's browser; the service's own pages and the sign-in pages (Google, Microsoft, Apple, Facebook, GitHub) stay in KaSH.

A contact's notifications are one: the last message, and how many are unread.
A conversation is marked as read without showing its service, from the Unread tab (a button over its row, or its context menu) or its notification, in WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord, Slack, Messenger, Google Chat, LinkedIn and Teams; in Instagram and Google Messages, which have no such action, by opening it in the background and going back to what was open.
A contact marked as priority (from the Unread tab, the search or Settings) has its unread conversations at the top of the Unread tab, and its notifications even in Do Not Disturb and when its service is muted.
Do Not Disturb turns on at the hours of its ranges (each with its days, and which may go past midnight), for all of KaSH (Settings › Notifications) or a service alone (its Edit window); during a range, the rail's bell lifts it until the range ends.
⌘1 to ⌘9 show the services of the rail, ⌘0 the Unread tab, ⇧⌘K the search.
In the service shown, ⌘R reloads its page, ⌘[ and ⌘] go back and forward, ⌥⌘I opens the developer tools; the menus of a service show these shortcuts.
Each service's page has its own zoom (⌘+ and ⌘−, or its "…" menu, which also brings it back to the default), and Settings › Appearance sets the pages' default zoom and the size of KaSH's own text, in percent.
KaSH's own notifications go away after a few seconds, or stay until they are closed (Settings › Notifications); how long macOS's stay is its own setting (banners or alerts), which Settings shows and opens.

The window opens again where it was, as big and in the mode it was in (floating, maximized, full screen) when KaSH was closed.


== Building

KaSH only builds and runs on macOS for now.

JCEF is built from sources, from the `jcef` git submodule: JetBrains publishes the jar of JCEF, but not its native libraries.
The build needs CMake, Ninja, pkg-config and a recent bison, which Homebrew provides:

[source,bash]
----
git submodule update --init --recursive
brew install cmake ninja pkgconf bison
./gradlew :app:run
----

The Xcode command line tools are enough: full Xcode is not needed.
Gradle downloads Apache Ant and the JetBrains Runtime itself.
The first build of JCEF downloads CEF and builds the dependencies of JCEF with vcpkg, which takes several minutes.

`./gradlew :app:createDistributable` makes `KaSH.app`, in `app/build/compose/binaries/main/app`.
The notifications of the macOS Notification Center and the launch at login only work in it.

[IMPORTANT]
====
KaSH needs a JetBrains Runtime *without* JCEF.
A JetBrains Runtime that has its own JCEF (`jbr_jcef`, `jbrsdk_jcef`) would use its classes instead of the ones built with KaSH's native libraries, and KaSH refuses to start on it.
====
